==========================================================================

2026-07-29 (~22:24-22:25, per file mtimes) -- BUILT: a targeted check of whether
  stage3a's reverse-engineered 6.25-9% relative-illuminance brightness floor agrees
  with the independent, literature-derived ABSOLUTE troland thresholds (Van Nes &
  Bouman 1967's 300 Td floor; CIE 191:2010's ~3-5 cd/m^2 boundary) found separately in
  this project's illumination literature review. These two pieces of work had never
  been checked against each other before this.

RESULT (not an abandonment -- a real, dated finding that PARTIALLY CONFIRMED the
existing approach and surfaced a separate bug):
  - At this project's own DEFAULT scene luminance (10,000 cd/m^2, used throughout every
    Stage 3/4 sweep), the two absolute floors and the 6.25-9% figure flatly DISAGREE --
    both absolute floors are cleared by an aperture far smaller than even the pure
    blur-only optimum, meaning brightness would not be a binding constraint at all
    under the project's own default assumption.
  - At a clinically-realistic luminance instead (100-300 cd/m^2, closer to how the
    actual clinical acuity studies this project validates against were likely
    conducted), Van Nes & Bouman's independently-derived floor lands at ~1128um at
    300 cd/m^2 -- close to the reverse-engineered 900-1250um range and inside the
    verified clinical bracket. Stated explicitly as corroboration, not a tuned match
    (the literature number was never adjusted to produce this).
  - SURFACED, NOT FIXED HERE: `DEFAULT_SCENE_LUMINANCE_CDM2=10000` in
    stage3_human_vision/photometry.py -- used throughout every Stage 3/4 sweep to
    date -- is flagged as likely the wrong luminance for comparing against clinical
    acuity data. This does not invalidate prior results as internally-consistent optics
    (relative aperture comparisons are unaffected by an overall luminance scale), but
    means none of them are directly comparable in absolute terms to clinical data.

STATUS: this specific finding is a completed, one-shot investigation (no evidence of a
later revision). Recommended next step, stated in FINDINGS.md and NOT done here: re-run
Stage 3/4's photometry-dependent sweeps at a clinically-realistic luminance (100-300
cd/m^2) and check whether Stage 4's failed acuity validation changes at all. Open
question not resolved: whether CIE or Van Nes & Bouman is the "correct" criterion for
THIS specific question (pinhole-restricted acuity) -- the two disagree with each other
by 2-3x, encoding different physiological criteria, not interchangeable numbers.
