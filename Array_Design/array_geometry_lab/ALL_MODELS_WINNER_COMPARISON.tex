\section*{The models}

Seven distinct ranking approaches were built over the course of this project. They differ on
three axes: which \emph{families} they consider, whether they apply a \emph{hard safety gate}
(reject unsafe designs outright) or a \emph{soft score} (penalize but never exclude), and what
\emph{objective} they rank survivors by.

\section*{Differences and reasons}

\subsection*{1. metric1 and FINAL\_WINNER are identical}
FINAL\_WINNER restricts the candidate pool to square/hexagonal/rectangular only; metric1 searches
all 10 families. They produce the \emph{same} winner at every M because square already wins
metric1's unrestricted search everywhere — the family restriction never actually bound. This
confirms square's dominance under packing\_density + margin-aware gating is not an artifact of
excluding other families.

\subsection*{2. S\_paper and S\_paper\_soft disagree with metric1 almost everywhere, and with each other}
Both are \emph{soft} scores — no candidate is ever excluded, only down-weighted. Investigated and
confirmed empirically (not assumed): $g$, the input to S\_paper's ghost term $\mu_g$, has
mean $\approx 419$ across this dataset's actual (spacing, blur) scale — always $\gg 1$ — so
$\mu_g=\text{sigmoid}(2(1-g))$ is numerically $<10^{-10}$ for \textbf{100\% of 8501 rows}. The
ghost term contributes nothing, safe or unsafe alike. What is left driving S\_paper's ranking is
mostly $\mu_\eta$ (closeness of fill-factor to the paper's target $\eta=0.15$) plus the coverage
term — properties orthogonal to whether a design actually ghosts. S\_paper\_soft additionally
swaps the coverage term for a fixed \emph{closed-form} blind-rate constant per family
($1-\pi/4\approx78.5\%$ open for square, $1-\pi/(2\sqrt3)\approx90.7\%$ for hex) — hexagonal's
better constant, combined with its own $\mu_\eta$ advantage, is enough to flip most M slots to
hexagonal even though this project's own independently-computed vignetting margin for that exact
hexagonal candidate is a real, decisive failure (\,$-7$ to $-20\,\mu$m of margin, verified directly
against \texttt{sweep\_results\_with\_density.csv}\,) at every M it wins. \textbf{Neither S\_paper
score has any vignetting term at all} — this is not a rounding issue, it is a structural gap in
the formula as specified.

\subsection*{3. The Shen Monte Carlo evaluator disagrees with S\_paper's own closed form}
Correlation(ghost\_rate, $g_\text{Sch}$) across all 7708 per-M candidates $= -0.0485$ — computed
directly, not assumed. ghost\_rate is bimodal (52.6\% of candidates at exactly 1.0, 10.9\% at
exactly 0.0); $g_\text{Sch}$ is a smooth unbounded number with no such structure. Shen's own MC
winner (archimedean\_spiral, M=2--6) independently reads ghost\_rate 0.47--0.91 under Shen's
\emph{own} more rigorous method — Shen's own published optimizer and Shen's own MC evaluator
disagree about Shen's own winning design. Separately, metric1's square winner shows an elevated
Shen ghost\_rate (0.38--0.82 at M=1--8) despite a real $+\infty$ margin under our own protocol —
explained entirely by Shen's much more permissive "any nonzero overlap admits a beam" admission
rule versus this project's own 50\%-overlap \texttt{active\_frac\_threshold}; not an actual safety
failure in our winner.

\subsection*{4. Effective density abandons square and hexagonal-by-\texorpdfstring{$S_\text{paper}$}{S\_paper} entirely}
metric1's packing\_density counts every generated hole, including ones that structurally never
admit light — confirmed: mean(effective\_density / packing\_density) $=0.175$ across the whole
dataset, i.e.\ packing\_density over-counted by $\approx5.7\times$ on average. Once ranking is
restricted to \emph{only} holes that actually admit light, square's dense-but-mostly-dark lattices
lose to \texttt{ring\_offset\_concentric} (M$\geq$2) and \texttt{hexagonal} (M=0/global) —
sparser designs whose few holes are each doing real work. This is the correct outcome of fixing a
metric that rewarded dark holes, not a defect.

\subsection*{5. \texorpdfstring{$c_\text{min}=55$}{c\_min=55} changes exactly one winner, and only after a real bug was fixed}
\texttt{poisson\_disk} (seed 7) initially won M=1.0 once Shen's perceptual floor made its margins
positive — but investigation found this used the single \emph{luckiest} seed of eight, only 2 of
which actually cleared both floored margins (worst-case ghost margin across seeds $=-0.138$,
seed-to-seed std $=0.220$, flagged \texttt{high\_seed\_variance} by this project's own established
$>1.0\times|\text{worst}|$ rule). Fixed by grouping stochastic candidates
(\texttt{poisson\_disk}, \texttt{jittered\_lattice}) by (family, N, spacing, M) and requiring the
\emph{worst} seed — not the best — to clear both margins before the group is even eligible, then
ranking eligible groups by mean effective density across seeds that also pass viability. Result:
\textbf{poisson\_disk loses}; every remaining stochastic group that is technically margin-eligible
still fails viability across every one of its seeds. The M=1.0 winner reverts to a deterministic
family (\texttt{square}, N=20, pitch=2000) — the only cell in the whole comparison where the
$c_\text{min}$ floor changes eff.\ density's own answer at all.

\subsection*{Summary}
No two of these seven models agree on a winner at every M. The disagreements are not noise —
each is traceable to one specific, confirmed mechanism: a saturated formula term (S\_paper/S\_paper\_soft's
$\mu_g$), a missing safety dimension (no vignetting term in either Shen score), a different beam-admission
threshold (Shen MC vs.\ this project's 50\% rule), a metric that counted dark holes as density
(packing\_density vs.\ effective\_density), or a single-lucky-seed selection bug (fixed, poisson\_disk
$\rightarrow$ square at M=1.0). This project's own hard-gated pipeline (metric1/FINAL\_WINNER) and its
coverage-corrected successor (effective density, seed-aware) are the two models built on real,
independently verified margins with an actual safety floor — the other five are useful comparison
points, not competing safety-verified answers.
