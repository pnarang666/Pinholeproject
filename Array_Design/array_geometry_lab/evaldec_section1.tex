\section{The Two Evaluation Models}

\textbf{Model A --- Worst-case gates} (this project's original primary
standard). A design must remain ghost-free and non-blind at every tested
gaze eccentricity $\varphi$ from 0$^\circ$ to 25$^\circ$. Pass/fail, no
weighting. Ranks survivors by effective density.

\textbf{Model B --- Gaze-weighted} (added later). Integrates the measured
eccentricity distribution over $\varphi$, sampling 36 azimuths per
eccentricity bin, and reports the \emph{fraction of actual viewing time}
a design spends blind or ghosting.

The two were built to differ in one intended dimension: whether rare
peripheral angles count as heavily as the frequently-used centre. They
turned out to differ in a second, unintended dimension as well --- and
that second difference is the important finding of this document.

\section{Critical Finding: Model A Is Not Actually Worst-Case}

Model A samples the pupil shift along a \textbf{single azimuthal
direction} ($+x$) as $\varphi$ increases. Model B samples 36 azimuths at
each eccentricity. Because holes are discrete objects with gaps between
them, a pupil can be fully lit when shifted in one direction and
completely dark when shifted the same distance in another.

Verified directly on the M=6 gaze-weighted winner
(\texttt{hexagonal}, N=100, pitch=4000\,$\mu$m, $a_{\rm hole}$=500\,$\mu$m):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Sampling method & Minimum illuminated area \\
\midrule
Single-axis ($+x$ only), as Model A uses & 181{,}457\,$\mu$m$^2$ (never blind) \\
All 36 azimuths, as Model B uses & \textbf{0\,$\mu$m$^2$ (blind at 3 of 936 samples)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\findbox{Across the full 8818-row candidate set, \textbf{1750 rows
(19.8\%)} are reported as ``never blind'' by Model A's
\texttt{min\_illuminated\_area\_across\_phi} while Model B's azimuthal
sampling finds genuine blindness --- in the worst case, up to
\textbf{20.3\% of viewing time}. This affects \emph{every} geometry family
tested (jittered\_lattice 641, rectangular 493, poisson\_disk 154, square
124, hexagonal 54, and all others). Model A is therefore \textbf{not
strictly more conservative than Model B}: it is stricter in eccentricity
(treating 25$^\circ$ as heavily as 0$^\circ$) but blind in azimuth.}
