\section{The question this task answers}
Does any rotated square/hexagonal/rectangular candidate beat
\texttt{jittered\_lattice}'s current winning density among structurally-safe
survivors, once lattice orientation (relative to this project's own established
anisotropic-gaze finding) is actually tested instead of assumed fixed at one
arbitrary angle?

\section{Headline result}
\textbf{No -- and the reversal is total, not marginal: \texttt{jittered\_lattice}
now wins at ZERO of 9 M-scopes} (it previously won 5 of 9: $M=1,2,3,6,8$). A
\textbf{rotated} \texttt{square} or \texttt{hexagonal} lattice now wins at 6 of 9
scopes ($M=1,2,3,6,8,$ global); \texttt{archimedean\_spiral} still wins
$M=4/5$; \texttt{rectangular} still wins $M=0$. \textbf{This is a real,
reportable change to the Metric-1 winner list, not a rounding effect} --
confirmed by direct inspection of the underlying gate values, not just the
summary density numbers (see Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}).

\section{Mechanism -- confirmed by direct inspection, not inferred from the summary alone}
\label{sec:mechanism}
\textbf{Density itself never changes with rotation} (rotating a rigid lattice
changes neither its packing density nor its ghost margin, which depends only on
pairwise distance). What changes is \textbf{which candidates pass the safety
gates} -- specifically \texttt{worst\_case\_vignetting\_margin}, exactly as this
task's own premise predicted (orientation relative to the anisotropic gaze axis
matters for vignetting, not for ghosting).

\subsection*{$M=1,2,3,6,8$ (square, pitch=3000um, $N=50$) -- clean, robust, unaffected by any numerical edge case}
Direct check of the underlying rows:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
rotation\_deg & worst\_case\_vignetting\_margin & structurally\_safe \\
\midrule
0.0  & $-5.939$ / $-7.050$ (varies by $M$) & False \\
15.0 & $-7.050$ & False \\
30.0 & $-7.050$ & False \\
45.0 & $+\infty$ & \textbf{True} $\leftarrow$ flips to safe \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\texttt{N\_actual} for this candidate is \textbf{perfectly stable across all 4
rotation angles} (20/20/20/20, 50/50/50/50, 100/100/100/100 -- verified
directly) -- this pitch (3000um) is far from the hole diameter (a\_hole=1250um at
$M=1$D, ratio=0.417), so no overlap-rejection boundary is anywhere near being
touched. This is a genuine geometric effect: rotating the lattice by 45 degrees
moves specific hole positions enough that no pair of holes is ever simultaneously
active under the pupil across the full gaze sweep
(\texttt{worst\_case\_vignetting\_margin} goes to a clean $+\infty$, not a
marginal small-positive value), where at 0/15/30 degrees a pair does become
active at some tested gaze angle and produces a firmly negative margin.
\textbf{High confidence finding.}

\subsection*{$M=$global (hexagonal, pitch=2000um, rotation=30$^\circ$) -- real, but flagged as numerically fragile}
Here \texttt{pitch\_um} (2000) exactly equals \texttt{a\_hole\_um} (2000, the
largest acuity-optimal aperture across the $M$ range, used for global-scope
overlap validity per this project's own established convention) -- \textbf{exact
tangency, ratio=1.0}, a case this project's own \texttt{compute\_density.py}
already documents as fragile: \texttt{reject\_overlapping()}'s strict $d^2 >
a_{\rm hole}^2$ test sits exactly on a floating-point knife-edge at exact
tangency. Direct check confirms \texttt{N\_actual} for this candidate actually
VARIES across rotation angles at the same $N_{\rm requested}$ (e.g.\
$N_{\rm requested}=20$ gives $N_{\rm actual}=7,9,7,9$ at rotation$=0/15/30/45$
respectively) -- a symptom of rotation's own floating-point rounding
(irrational $\cos$/$\sin$ values) nudging which near-tied points survive the
exact-tangency boundary, not a bug in the rotation transform itself (verified
elsewhere that rotation preserves \texttt{N\_actual} exactly away from this
boundary -- see Section~\ref{sec:order}).

\textbf{The $M=$global winner-change is real for the specific rotation angles
tested} (rotation=30$^\circ$ genuinely reaches \texttt{structurally\_safe=True}
with \texttt{worst\_case\_vignetting\_margin}$=+\infty$ at this pitch),
\textbf{but it should be read as ``a numerically narrow window this project's
own known exact-tangency edge case opened up,'' not as a broad, robustly-safe
orientation} the way the $M=1$--8 finding is. Recommend re-checking this
specific candidate at a pitch slightly off exact tangency (e.g.\ 1990 or
2010um) before treating it as a settled design choice.

\section{Step 1 order requirement: rotation\_deg=0.0 exact-reproduction, confirmed before the sweep ran}
\label{sec:order}
Per this task's explicit ORDER requirement, \texttt{spot\_check\_generators.py}
was extended and run BEFORE the rotation sweep: \texttt{rotation\_deg=0.0}
reproduces the pre-rotation-support output bit-for-bit (\texttt{np.array\_equal},
not just \texttt{np.allclose}) for all three families, and
\texttt{rotation\_deg=30.0} was independently verified to (a) actually change
coordinates, (b) preserve \texttt{N\_actual} exactly, and (c) preserve every
pairwise distance exactly -- all PASS. (The one place \texttt{N\_actual} does
vary with rotation in the real sweep, described above, is the pre-existing
exact-tangency edge case, confirmed by direct inspection to be a
floating-point boundary effect specific to \texttt{pitch\_um==a\_hole\_um}, not
a contradiction of this check -- the spot-check's own $N=50$/pitch=900/a\_hole=400
case is nowhere near that boundary.)

\section{What was changed, and what was preserved}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{generators/base\_generator.py}: added
  \texttt{rotate\_points(coords\_um, rotation\_deg)} (shared helper, avoids
  duplicating the same rotation matrix in 3 files). \texttt{reject\_overlapping},
  \texttt{recenter\_on\_centroid}, \texttt{finalize} unchanged.
  \item \texttt{generators/square.py}, \texttt{generators/hexagonal.py},
  \texttt{generators/rectangular.py}: added \texttt{rotation\_deg=0.0} parameter,
  applied before \texttt{bg.finalize()}. Core lattice construction (meshgrid,
  radial sort) unchanged.
  \item \texttt{sweep/run\_rotation\_sweep.py} (new): re-swept ONLY
  square/hexagonal/rectangular, ONLY adding rotation\_deg in $\{0,15,30,45\}$ on
  the EXISTING N/spacing grid -- no other family touched or re-run.
  \item \texttt{sweep\_results.csv}: original preserved as
  \texttt{sweep\_results\_PREROTATION\_legacy.csv} (6395 rows, untouched).
  Existing square/hexagonal/rectangular rows retroactively tagged with
  \texttt{;rotation\_deg=0.0} in their \texttt{spacing\_params} string (text-only,
  no numeric column touched). New rows for rotation in $\{15,30,45\}$ appended.
  \textbf{No duplicate rows} -- verified by exact-duplicate count matching the
  number of existing tagged rows precisely (702), not assumed; a first attempt at
  this check found a real bug (an \texttt{M}-column string/float dtype mismatch
  from the CSV round-trip silently broke \texttt{drop\_duplicates()}, caught
  because the dedup count didn't match the expected value and was investigated
  before proceeding). Final: 8933 rows.
  \item \texttt{sweep\_results\_with\_density.csv}: re-run via the UNMODIFIED
  \texttt{degeneracy\_filter.py}/\texttt{compute\_density.py}. Previous version
  preserved as \texttt{sweep\_results\_with\_density\_PREROTATION\_legacy.csv}.
  432 rows excluded (108 designs x 4 rotation angles -- the same known-degenerate
  combinations recurring per rotation, since the degeneracy criteria are
  rotation-invariant). \texttt{open\_area\_fraction<=1.0001} assertion:
  \textbf{passed, zero violations}, 8501 surviving rows.
  \item \texttt{comparison\_metric/\allowbreak metric1\_max\_density\_per\_M.csv}: re-run via
  the UNMODIFIED \texttt{gates.py}/\texttt{metrics.py} (Metric 1 only, via the new
  \texttt{comparison\_metric/\allowbreak run\_metric1\_only.py}). Previous version preserved
  as \texttt{metric1\_max\_density\_per\_M\_PREROTATION\_legacy.csv}.
  \item Untouched, as required: every other family's data (concentric,
  fibonacci\_spiral, golden\_angle, archimedean\_spiral, ring\_offset\_concentric,
  poisson\_disk, jittered\_lattice), \texttt{array\_optimization/}, and every
  Phase 2 confirmation file.
\end{itemize}
