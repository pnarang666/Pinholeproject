\section{Which to Use for Fabrication}

Nine full designs is more than a first physical test needs. Two candidates
cover the practically useful range and are the ones this project recommends
building first.

\recbox{
\textbf{1. \texttt{square}, pitch=2000\,$\mu$m, N=20, a\_hole=1250\,$\mu$m (M=1).}
This is the single most informative design to test physically: it is the
only winner with a real, finite, measured safety margin rather than a
structural zero-active-pair guarantee. A physical measurement here directly
tests whether the model's finite-margin prediction (0.128) holds up in
reality --- the structural-safety designs cannot tell you this, since they
are safe by geometry regardless of small modeling errors.

\medskip
\textbf{2. \texttt{ring\_offset\_concentric}, ring\_spacing=4000\,$\mu$m, N=20
(M=2 through 8).} One physical frame, six aperture-size inserts (750, 600,
500, 400\,$\mu$m) covers the entire higher-myopia range with a single hole
pattern. Lowest fabrication cost per M-level covered of any option in the
table.
}

\subsection{Why Not the Others First}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{M=0's ring\_offset\_concentric} and \textbf{global's
  hexagonal} are both structurally safe with wide margin --- lower priority
  for a first physical test than the finite-margin M=1 design, since they
  are less likely to reveal a gap between prediction and reality.
  \item All designs in this table are already the survivors of an extensive
  audit (density-metric bias, boundary blackout cases, and a seed-lottery
  defect were each found and fixed against earlier candidates) --- none of
  the remaining seven "not recommended first" designs carry a known defect,
  they are simply lower priority for the first fabrication round.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Practical Note}
The M=1 design (1250\,$\mu$m holes) and the M=2--8 design family (750--400\,$\mu$m
holes) together span the full aperture range needed for laser-cut shim
fabrication testing described elsewhere in this project --- test the smallest
hole size (400\,$\mu$m, M=8 insert) first to confirm cutting-edge quality
before committing material to the full set.
