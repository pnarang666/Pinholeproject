ExP3 FROSTED SCREEN -- PLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY
==========================================================================

METHOD (confirmed, not assumed -- see analyze_exp3.py docstring): the
intensity image is standard BT.601 grayscale (cv2.cvtColor ... GRAY), NOT
a naive max(R,G,B) or plain channel-mean -- both of those were tried and
gave the WRONG numbers; BT.601 grayscale is the one that reproduces this
task's own hand-verified 1000um reference (45.6px at 0mm, 63.6px at
20mm) essentially exactly (this pipeline recovers 45.58px and 63.60px on
the same files). Threshold = 50% of that image's peak; spot = the LARGEST
connected component of the threshold mask only (hardened per this task's
own instruction); radius_px = sqrt(area_px/pi).

LIMITATION, stated explicitly: displacement_mm is NOT converted to
diopters anywhere in this report. That conversion needs the beam width at
the lens calibrated, which has not been done. All results below are in mm
of screen displacement and pixels only.

5 files excluded outright (not measured): 20260902_*.jpg timestamped
setup/calibration shots -- see measurements/excluded_files.csv.

CROSS-APERTURE SLOPE CHECK -- does larger aperture show a steeper
radius-vs-displacement slope, as geometric defocus blur (scales with
aperture diameter) predicts?
  600um: slope=0.601 px/mm
  800um: slope=0.654 px/mm
  500um: slope=0.658 px/mm
  1400um: slope=0.676 px/mm
  400um: slope=0.718 px/mm
  1000um: slope=0.882 px/mm

VERDICT: the slope ordering (600, 800, 500, 1400, 400, 1000) does NOT
match the predicted aperture-size ordering (400, 500, 600, 800, 1000, 1400).
Reported plainly -- not smoothed over or explained away.

Context, not an excuse: 5 of 6 apertures (400-1400um, excluding 1000um's
high 0.882 px/mm) cluster in a narrow
0.601-0.718 px/mm band -- a weak
aperture-dependence signal here would be easy to scramble by real rig noise
between aperture swaps. This project's own centroid_drift_px column already
shows substantial (100-270px) drift building up with displacement on EVERY
aperture, confirming the rig is not perfectly axis-stable between shots. That
is a plausible contributing factor, not a confirmed explanation -- the ordering
mismatch is reported as a real result, not attributed to noise by assumption.
