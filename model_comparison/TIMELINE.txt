==========================================================================

2026-07-27 (~14:52-14:56, per file mtimes) -- BUILT: a three-way benchmark of "how
  blurry is the retinal image for a given aperture" -- Model 1 (exact ASM,
  cornea-only, wraps stage1_v2/), Model 2 (exact ASM x2 + lens phase + axial-myopia
  model, wraps stage2/), and Model 3 (the OLD closed-form formula chain from both
  reference handbooks, including the classical root-sum-square r_total =
  sqrt(r_geo^2+r_diff^2) combination) -- compared against each other and against
  real-world clinical/commercial/literature reference values.

FINDING (this IS the "which approach we abandoned and why" story for this folder):
  Model 3's closed-form RSS-combination formula diverges from Model 2's exact
  numerical r_EE(50%) result by 64.8% MEAN and up to 192.8% MAX, worst specifically at
  mild myopia (M=1D), across every point on the shared comparison grid. Root cause
  identified, not just measured: blur sources actually combine by complex-field
  CONVOLUTION under real wave propagation, not by root-sum-square quadrature -- RSS is
  a geometric-optics-style approximation that does not hold once diffraction and
  defocus interact in a real near-field propagation.

STATUS: Model 3 (the classical RSS/closed-form chain) is confirmed SUPERSEDED/
DEPRECATED for any quantitative use -- kept in this folder only as the explicit
benchmark baseline the exact models (1 and 2) are compared against, not as a
recommended computation method. Models 1 and 2 (exact ASM propagation) are the
adopted, currently-trusted approach project-wide. Full detail: model_comparison/
README.md.
