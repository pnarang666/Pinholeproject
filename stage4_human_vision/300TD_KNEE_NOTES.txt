300 Td BRIGHTNESS KNEE — WHAT IT IS, WHERE IT'S USED, WHAT IT ACTUALLY DOES
============================================================================
Companion note to acuity.py's brightness_logmar_correction(). Read this before
citing the brightness term in the presentation.


1. WHAT THE KNEE IS
--------------------
Retinal illuminance threshold, in trolands (Td), separating two regimes of
contrast detection:

  >= 300 Td  (Weber-Fechner regime)
    Threshold contrast modulation is illuminance-independent. Being brighter
    than this does not make fine detail any easier to see.

  <  300 Td  (de Vries-Rose regime)
    Threshold contrast rises as illuminance^-0.5. Below the knee, every drop
    in light costs real acuity.

Source: Van Nes & Bouman (1967), J Opt Soc Am 57(3):401-406. Verified
directly against the primary abstract (see constants.json citation block
and acuity.py's docstring) -- not a secondary summary.


2. THE FORMULA (acuity.py: brightness_logmar_correction)
----------------------------------------------------------
    delta_bright = 0                                  if Td >= 300
    delta_bright = 0.060 * log10(300 / Td)             if Td <  300

  0.060 = logmar_per_log10_illuminance
          Source: Tidbury, Czanner & Newsham (2016), verified against
          their Table 3 (p<0.001). An independently-derived alternative
          slope from Sheedy, Bailey & Raasch (1984), ~0.0664, could only
          be traced to a secondary summary -- Tidbury's directly-verified
          value is what's wired in.

  300   = weber_knee_td (constants.json -> brightness_acuity.weber_knee_td)

delta_bright is ADDED to logMAR (additive in log space, not multiplicative
in MAR-arcmin space). It only ever makes acuity worse (delta >= 0), never
better -- there is no negative branch above the knee.


3. HOW Td ITSELF IS COMPUTED (illuminance.py)
------------------------------------------------
    effective_stop_mm = min(natural_pupil_diameter_mm, pinhole_diameter_mm)
    Td = scene_luminance_cdm2 * pupil_area_mm2(effective_stop_mm)

  scene_luminance_cdm2 defaults to 10,000 cd/m^2
  (constants.json -> pipeline.default_scene_luminance_cdm2)
  = bright outdoor daylight on a white surface. NOT indoor room lighting.

  Natural pupil diameter comes from the Watson-Yellott (2012) model,
  solved iteratively against the illuminance itself (pupil.py).

  For every fabricated design in this project the pinhole is smaller than
  the natural pupil, so the pinhole IS the effective stop -- this is the
  whole physical point of the device, and it's why aperture diameter alone
  determines Td at fixed scene luminance.

5. WHAT THIS MEANS -- SAY THIS FIRST, DON'T LET IT BE FOUND
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The brightness term is real, correctly cited, correctly implemented, and
currently inert for your headline results. It is not doing any work in
the numbers you're presenting. It WOULD activate:
  - for any aperture below 200 um, or
  - for a measurement taken at lower scene luminance (e.g. indoors,
    typically 100-300 cd/m^2 -- see section 7 below).

Defensible line: "The brightness correction is cited and implemented, but
at our assumed outdoor luminance it's zero for every aperture we actually
selected -- our optimum is set by the MTF10 cutoff, not by illuminance.
It would bind indoors, which is why we specify the measurement luminance
before any acuity test."


6. *** THE ACUITY MODEL FAILED ITS OWN VALIDATION -- READ BEFORE CITING ANY
   PREDICTED LOGMAR NUMBER ***
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Source: stage4_human_vision/RESULTS.md (Section 2) and
        stage4_human_vision/LUMINANCE_RECONCILIATION.md

The full acuity predictor (MTF10 + VSOTF + this brightness term) was
validated against Wang et al. 2009's real clinical pinhole-acuity data.
Result:

  M (D)   R^2      Pearson r   p-value
  -----   ------   ---------   -------
  0       -6.39    0.395       0.510
  2       -4.98    0.393       0.513
  5       -2.84    0.405       0.499

Negative R^2 = worse than predicting the mean every time. p ~ 0.50 = no
significant relationship detected. Every one of the 15 tested (aperture,
M) points showed the SAME-DIRECTION bias -- the model over-predicts blur
(worse logMAR than observed) at every point, not just weak scatter.

The luminance assumption was tested as a possible cause and RULED OUT:
correcting to Wang 2009's own stated illumination (15 foot-candles ->
43.7 cd/m^2, replacing the 10,000 cd/m^2 default) made R^2 WORSE
(-7.88 vs -6.68 at M=0), not better -- because it pushed apertures below
the 300 Td knee and added predicted blur on top of a model that was
already over-predicting it.

RESULTS.md's own diagnosis, most-likely cause first:
  1. Uncalibrated combination -- acuity.py's VSOTF/brightness formula has
     no fitted offset or scale against real data (DESIGN.md flags this
     as a known "requires empirical calibration" gap).
  2. The Wang 2009 dataset itself is non-monotonic at n=5: observed
     logMAR = 0.28, 0.31, 1.00, 0.40, 0.50 at 500/750/1000/1200/1500 um --
     a spike at 1000um that reverses at 1200um. A single outlier at n=5
     will sink any smooth model regardless of which optical metric
     underlies it.
  3. No per-subject refractive error recorded in the dataset -- comparing
     one simulated M against subjects with unstated real M adds
     irreducible noise.
  4. n=5 per M is too small for p<0.05 even with real signal present.

RESULTS.md's own conclusion: this "cannot be properly validated with
currently available public data." A fourth predictor variant was
deliberately not chased, since (1)-(3) would sink any smooth model built
on this project's optics against this specific dataset -- this reads as
a dataset/task-mismatch problem, not proof the underlying optics are
wrong.

WHAT TO DO WITH THIS AT PRESENTATION:
  - Do NOT present "+0.33 logMAR at M=8D" as a validated clinical
    prediction. It is a self-consistent model output that has not been
    externally validated, and the one external check available found no
    significant relationship to real data.
  - DO say this yourself, in this order, if the acuity model comes up:
    the model is internally consistent (traceable, cited, each term
    justified) but externally unvalidated; the one validation attempt
    against Wang 2009 failed (R^2 negative, p~0.5); the luminance
    assumption was tested and ruled out as the cause; the leading
    suspects are lack of calibration and the small, non-monotonic
    clinical dataset, not necessarily the optics.
  - This is exactly why the two-pinhole / logMAR measurement protocol
    (LOGMAR_ACUITY_PROTOCOL.pdf) matters more than it might otherwise --
    it is your own calibration data, on your own device, which the model
    currently lacks entirely.
